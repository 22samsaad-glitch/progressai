What we look at

The parts of your day that still run by hand.

These are the things we look at: how work actually moves through your shop, and which parts a machine could handle. Not every one of these should be automated. Part of the assessment is telling you which ones to leave alone. You probably already know roughly where the time goes. What's harder is knowing which of it is worth handing to a machine, and what that would actually involve.

A typical example

The call that comes in while you're under a furnace.

Here's one of the most common things we find, and what changing it actually looks like.

Today

Nobody leaves a voicemail anymore. They call the next name on the list, and you never know it happened. That's the part that bothers most owners: not the lost job, the not knowing.

After

A text goes out in seconds saying you're on a job and will call back. The customer waits instead of dialing the next number. You still handle the call yourself, just on your schedule rather than theirs.

Why this is different

Your name is on the truck.

There's no call centre between you and the customer. No one to catch it if a message goes out wrong at 9pm. That's why most of what we recommend starts small on purpose: after hours only, or only when nobody picks up after four rings. You decide how far it goes, and we'll tell you when we think it should stop.
